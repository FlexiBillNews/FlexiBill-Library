# Merchant Growth, Adoption and Expansion

**Playbook:** Commercial Strategy & Sales Enablement  
**Document:** 09 — Merchant Growth, Adoption and Expansion  
**Audience:** DOKU Sales, Account Management, Merchant Success, Product, Operations, and Management  
**Status:** Internal working playbook — proposed operating framework; product and commercial validation required

---

## 1. Purpose

This playbook defines how DOKU teams grow FlexiBill merchants after onboarding and initial activation.

The objective is to move beyond merchant acquisition and establish a structured approach to:

1. Drive successful merchant activation.
2. Increase adoption of relevant FlexiBill capabilities.
3. Improve billing and payment performance.
4. Identify and reduce revenue leakage.
5. Expand payment volume and eligible use cases.
6. Improve merchant retention.
7. Develop long-term strategic relationships.

**Core principle:**

> A merchant is not successful simply because it has signed a contract or completed integration. Success is demonstrated through sustained usage, measurable business outcomes, and growing value for both the merchant and DOKU.

---

## 2. Merchant lifecycle and growth model

Manage merchants through six lifecycle stages.

| Stage | Merchant status | Primary objective |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Onboarding | Contracted, implementation underway | Prepare for launch |
| 2. Activation | First successful billing and payment, where applicable | Establish real usage |
| 3. Adoption | Regular billing activity | Build operating consistency |
| 4. Optimization | Performance and operational review | Improve outcomes |
| 5. Expansion | Additional volume, products, or entities | Grow value |
| 6. Strategic partnership | Mature relationship | Joint business development |

### Lifecycle transition criteria

A merchant should progress based on evidence, not elapsed time alone.

Examples:
- Onboarding → Activation: agreed production acceptance criteria met.
- Activation → Adoption: repeat billing activity demonstrated.
- Adoption → Optimization: sufficient operating data available.
- Optimization → Expansion: validated incremental opportunity identified.
- Expansion → Strategic partnership: sustained mutual business value demonstrated.

Define the actual activity thresholds for each merchant segment.

## 3. Merchant success framework

Measure merchant success across five dimensions.

| Dimension | Core question | Example indicators |
|---|---|---|
| Activation | Has the merchant started using FlexiBill? | First billing, first successful payment |
| Adoption | Is usage becoming consistent? | Active billing cycles, repeat volume |
| Performance | Is the billing process working effectively? | Collection success, recovery, exceptions |
| Value | Is the solution producing business benefit? | Reduced manual work, improved visibility |
| Growth | Is the relationship expanding? | Volume growth, additional use cases, entities |

### Merchant health status

Use a practical health classification.

**Healthy**
- Regular usage.
- Stable billing operations.
- Agreed payment workflows functioning.
- Merchant engagement maintained.
- Growth opportunity identified.

**Needs attention**
- Declining billing activity.
- Increasing failed or overdue billing.
- Unresolved operational issues.
- Low adoption of agreed workflows.
- Weak merchant engagement.

**At risk**
- No meaningful production activity.
- Significant billing or integration failures.
- Merchant dissatisfaction.
- Material unresolved commercial or operational issues.
- Possible migration or termination.

The classification should be supported by measurable evidence and an assigned account owner.

## 4. Merchant onboarding-to-adoption plan

### First 30 days after go-live

Objectives:
- Confirm production workflow.
- Validate first billing and payment outcomes.
- Monitor exceptions.
- Confirm merchant understanding of operational processes.
- Ensure support and reconciliation processes work.

Required activities:
1. Conduct launch review.
2. Confirm first successful billing.
3. Validate payment status reporting.
4. Review failed and pending outcomes.
5. Confirm billing references and reconciliation.
6. Record merchant feedback.
7. Assign outstanding actions.

### Days 31–60

Objectives:
- Establish repeat usage.
- Identify early performance trends.
- Reduce avoidable operational exceptions.
- Confirm reporting quality.

Required activities:
1. Review billing volume and value.
2. Compare actual activity with forecast.
3. Review collection success.
4. Analyze failure reasons.
5. Review customer support issues.
6. Identify process improvements.
7. Confirm next adoption milestone.

### Days 61–90

Objectives:
- Establish a stable operating rhythm.
- Identify optimization opportunities.
- Develop an expansion plan.

Required activities:
1. Review the first complete operating period.
2. Assess billing and payment performance.
3. Review recovery and overdue billing.
4. Assess active customer or subscriber trends where data permits.
5. Identify additional eligible use cases.
6. Agree merchant growth objectives.
7. Produce a 90-day business review.

Actual review windows should align with the merchant's billing frequency and operating model.

## 5. Merchant adoption measurement

### Core adoption metrics

| Metric | Definition | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Activated merchants | Merchants meeting agreed activation criteria | Measures successful launch |
| Active merchants | Activated merchants with qualifying activity in the defined period | Measures ongoing use |
| Active billing use cases | Billing workflows with qualifying activity | Measures product adoption |
| Unique billing obligations | Distinct billing obligations created | Measures workflow usage |
| Successful billing obligations | Unique obligations successfully collected | Measures outcomes |
| Processed payment volume | Eligible payment value processed | Measures business contribution |
| Repeat usage | Merchants with activity in consecutive periods | Measures consistency |
| Merchant retention | Merchants retained over a defined cohort period | Measures relationship health |
| Expansion rate | Merchants adding qualifying volume or use cases | Measures growth |

### Important measurement rules

- Define whether merchant activity is measured by billing creation, payment attempt, or successful collection.
- Do not count test transactions as production adoption.
- Define treatment of duplicate records.
- Separate payment attempts from unique billing obligations.
- Specify currency and reporting timezone.
- Define the active-period threshold.
- Separate paused, seasonal, and terminated merchants.

## 6. Identify merchant growth opportunities

Use six expansion dimensions.

### A. Volume expansion

Potential opportunities:
- Increase the number of billing obligations processed.
- Move more eligible customer populations to the FlexiBill workflow.
- Expand billing coverage across existing merchant branches.
- Migrate additional billing cycles.

Validate incremental eligible volume and the merchant's ability to operationalize the change.

### B. Payment-method expansion

Potential opportunities:
- Introduce additional supported payment methods.
- Improve the payment-method mix.
- Expand recurring authorization where eligible.
- Review payment performance by method.

All payment methods must be validated for the merchant, country, use case, and applicable authorization requirements.

### C. Use-case expansion

Potential opportunities:
- New subscription or membership products.
- Additional billing frequencies.
- Installment collection.
- Education or membership fee collection.
- Insurance premium collection.
- Corporate or B2B billing.

Every additional use case requires a separate capability and feasibility assessment.

### D. Customer-base expansion

Potential opportunities:
- Additional merchant customer segments.
- New membership populations.
- Additional business units.
- New merchant locations or branches.

Validate customer data, authorization, operating ownership, and commercial scope.

### E. Integration expansion

Potential opportunities:
- Move from manual workflows to supported batch processing.
- Introduce API / H2H integration.
- Extend an existing Hybrid workflow.
- Connect additional merchant systems.

Do not recommend a more complex integration pattern unless it solves a defined merchant problem.

### F. Partnership and ecosystem expansion

Potential opportunities:
- Vertical software partners.
- Banking and financial institution partnerships.
- Distribution channels.
- Industry platforms.
- Technology partners.

Define partner responsibilities, commercial terms, technical dependencies, and support ownership.

## 7. Revenue leakage and growth assessment

A merchant growth review should include a structured leakage assessment.

### Billing leakage

Examples:
- Expected billing obligations not generated.
- Duplicate or missing billing records.
- Incorrect amounts.
- Incorrect due dates.
- Billing configuration errors.

### Payment leakage

Examples:
- Initial payment failures.
- Authorization problems.
- Unsupported payment methods.
- Unresolved pending transactions.
- Failed payment status synchronization.

### Recovery leakage

Examples:
- Initially unsuccessful billing not followed up.
- Eligible retry not performed.
- Customer not informed of payment failure.
- Payment details not updated where a supported process exists.
- Overdue billing not managed.

### Operational leakage

Examples:
- Manual reconciliation exceptions.
- Duplicate processing.
- Missing references.
- Delayed payment-status updates.
- Incomplete reporting.

### Leakage assessment template

| Dimension | Metric | Baseline | Opportunity | Owner |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Billing | Expected vs generated obligations | To establish | To quantify | Merchant |
| Collection | Initial billing-level success | To establish | To quantify | Operations |
| Recovery | Eligible recovery rate | To establish | To quantify | Operations |
| Reconciliation | Unmatched payment records | To establish | To quantify | Finance |
| Adoption | Eligible billing population using FlexiBill | To establish | To quantify | Account owner |

### Calculation discipline

\[
\text{Billing Coverage}
=
\frac{\text{Generated Eligible Billing Obligations}}
{\text{Expected Eligible Billing Obligations}}
\times 100\%
\]

\[
\text{Billing-Level Collection Success}
=
\frac{\text{Unique Successfully Collected Obligations}}
{\text{Eligible Unique Billing Obligations}}
\times 100\%
\]

\[
\text{Recovery Rate}
=
\frac{\text{Eligible Initially Failed Obligations Subsequently Recovered}}
{\text{Eligible Initially Failed Obligations}}
\times 100\%
\]

Use consistent cohort definitions and observation cutoffs. Do not include unresolved pending transactions as failed unless the agreed status rules support that treatment.

## 9. Merchant health scorecard

Use a weighted model as a starting point. Adjust weights based on merchant segment and business model.

| Dimension | Illustrative weight |
|---|---:|
| Production usage and adoption | 25% |
| Billing and payment performance | 25% |
| Operational stability | 20% |
| Merchant engagement | 15% |
| Commercial performance and growth | 15% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

### Scoring guide

| Score | Interpretation |
|---:|---|
| 5 | Strong and improving |
| 4 | Healthy |
| 3 | Stable but improvement needed |
| 2 | Weak or declining |
| 1 | Critical |

\[
\text{Merchant Health Score}
=
\sum(\text{Dimension Score}\times\text{Dimension Weight})
\]

Use a consistent score scale, and document the evidence behind every score.

Suggested interpretation:
- 4.0–5.0: Healthy.
- 3.0–3.9: Monitor.
- 2.0–2.9: Needs intervention.
- Below 2.0: At risk.

These are proposed thresholds, not an existing DOKU service standard.

## 10. Expansion opportunity scoring

Evaluate opportunities before investing significant resources.

| Dimension | Illustrative weight |
|---|---:|
| Incremental payment volume | 25% |
| Merchant strategic value | 20% |
| Product and technical feasibility | 20% |
| Commercial contribution | 20% |
| Implementation readiness | 15% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

Score each dimension from 1 to 5.

\[
\text{Opportunity Score}
=
\sum(\text{Dimension Score}\times\text{Dimension Weight})
\]

Prioritize opportunities that combine:
- Meaningful incremental value.
- Strong product fit.
- Manageable delivery complexity.
- Merchant readiness.
- Sustainable economics.

A high commercial score must not override a mandatory product or compliance gap.

## 11. Merchant retention and churn prevention

### Early warning indicators

- Declining billing volume.
- Declining number of active billing obligations.
- Repeated payment failures.
- Increasing reconciliation exceptions.
- Unresolved technical incidents.
- Reduced merchant engagement.
- Delayed invoice or payment disputes.
- Increasing competitor engagement.
- Merchant requests to reduce or terminate scope.

### Retention intervention

| Risk | Intervention |
|---|---|
| Declining usage | Understand business or technical cause |
| Collection problems | Analyze failure reasons and eligible recovery |
| Integration instability | Coordinate technical remediation |
| Poor reconciliation | Review references and reporting |
| Commercial pressure | Review total value and approved options |
| Product gap | Document requirement and escalation |
| Low engagement | Re-establish business review cadence |
| Competitor threat | Understand the actual switching driver |

Do not treat discounts as the default retention solution.

### Churn analysis

Record:
- Merchant termination date.
- Last active date.
- Last billing activity.
- Last successful payment.
- Primary churn reason.
- Lost payment volume.
- Lost DOKU revenue.
- Replacement provider, if known.
- Recovery or reactivation possibility.

## 13. Cross-sell and expansion conversation

Use a consultative approach.

### Suggested questions

**Billing coverage**
- Which billing populations are not currently using FlexiBill?
- Are there other recurring products or services?

**Payment coverage**
- Are there supported payment methods that could improve customer access?
- Are there additional payment workflows to evaluate?

**Operational improvement**
- Which billing activities still require manual work?
- Where do reconciliation exceptions occur?
- What happens when a customer misses a payment?

**Customer experience**
- Which customer billing interactions create the most friction?
- Are customer self-service requirements increasing?

**Growth**
- Are you launching new products or branches?
- Are you entering new markets?
- Are you planning to increase customer acquisition?

Use the answers to create a separately validated opportunity, not an assumed product commitment.

## 17. First 90-day merchant growth action plan

### Days 1–30: Stabilize

- Confirm merchant production usage.
- Validate billing and payment reporting.
- Resolve critical operational issues.
- Establish baseline metrics.
- Agree account review cadence.

**Output:** Stable merchant operating baseline.

### Days 31–60: Optimize

- Analyze collection and recovery performance.
- Review reconciliation exceptions.
- Identify billing process improvements.
- Assess usage consistency.
- Develop a prioritized growth opportunity list.

**Output:** Merchant optimization plan.

### Days 61–90: Expand

- Prioritize one or more feasible growth opportunities.
- Validate product and technical scope.
- Prepare an approved commercial proposal if required.
- Agree expansion milestones.
- Establish next-quarter targets.

**Output:** Approved merchant expansion plan or documented decision to defer.
