# Pricing and Commercial Conversation Guide

**Playbook:** Commercial Strategy & Sales Enablement  
**Document:** 06 — Pricing and Commercial Conversation Guide  
**Audience:** DOKU Sales, Presales, Account Management, Partnership, Finance, and Management  
**Status:** Internal working playbook — commercial policy and pricing approval required

---

## 1. Purpose

This playbook provides a consistent approach to discussing FlexiBill pricing and commercial value with merchants.

The objective is to help commercial teams:

1. Understand the merchant's billing and payment economics.
2. Explain the commercial value of FlexiBill.
3. Structure a transparent pricing discussion.
4. Distinguish payment processing fees from other potential charges.
5. Build a defensible merchant business case.
6. Handle pricing objections without immediately discounting.
7. Protect DOKU's commercial sustainability.
8. Ensure all external pricing commitments are approved.

**Core principle:**

> Sell the economic value of the billing and payment solution, not simply the lowest transaction fee.

This playbook is a discussion framework. It does not establish official DOKU tariffs, discount authority, contractual terms, or country-specific pricing.

## 2. Understand the commercial model

FlexiBill is a payment-connected billing orchestration solution. Its commercial value can come from several components.

| Value component | Merchant benefit | Potential DOKU commercial consideration |
|---|---|---|
| Billing automation | Reduced manual billing activity | Platform or service pricing, if approved |
| Payment processing | Collection through supported payment methods | Payment processing fees |
| Recurring collection | More structured recurring payment operations | Applicable payment fees |
| Billing visibility | Better operational monitoring | Included or separately priced service, subject to policy |
| Integration | Connection with merchant systems | Setup or integration services, if applicable |
| Customer billing experience | Easier access to billing and payment journeys | Included or separately priced capability, subject to policy |
| Recovery operations | Better visibility into failed and overdue billing | Applicable processing and service charges |

Do not assume that every component is independently chargeable. The actual commercial model must follow approved DOKU policy.

## 3. Establish the merchant's commercial baseline

Before discussing fees, understand the merchant's current business economics.

### Merchant billing profile

| Metric | Required information |
|---|---|
| Monthly billing count | Number of unique billing obligations |
| Monthly billing value | Total amount due |
| Average ticket size | Average amount per billing obligation |
| Collection frequency | Daily, weekly, monthly, annual, or custom |
| Payment mix | Cards, bank channels, wallets, or other supported methods |
| Initial collection success | Successful billing obligations / eligible billing obligations |
| Recovery rate | Recovered initially unsuccessful billing obligations / eligible initially unsuccessful billing obligations |
| Manual operating cost | Staff time and process cost |
| Current provider fees | Existing payment and platform charges |
| Integration cost | Internal and external implementation effort |

Record the data source, reporting period, currency, and calculation method.

### Discovery questions

Ask the merchant:

- How many billing obligations do you generate each month?
- What is the average billing amount?
- How much billing value is successfully collected?
- How much remains unpaid?
- What happens when the initial payment attempt fails?
- How much manual effort is required?
- What fees are currently paid across billing and payment providers?
- What is the expected growth in billing volume?
- Which commercial model would be easiest for your finance team to manage?

## 4. Understand the pricing components

Use this structure to organize a commercial discussion.

### A. Payment processing fees

Fees associated with successfully processed payment transactions, subject to the applicable payment method, merchant category, country, and approved commercial agreement.

Potential dimensions include:
- Percentage-based fees.
- Fixed transaction fees.
- Payment-method-specific fees.
- Refund or reversal treatment, where applicable.
- Settlement-related charges, where applicable.

Never quote a fee without confirming the current approved tariff and its applicability to the merchant.

### B. Platform or service fees

A separate platform or service fee may be considered only where an approved DOKU commercial model supports it.

Possible commercial structures to evaluate internally:
- Monthly subscription fee.
- Tiered platform fee.
- Usage-based service fee.
- Contracted enterprise package.

These are commercial design options, not a statement that each fee model is currently available.

### C. Implementation and integration fees

Depending on approved policy and scope, a project may include:
- Technical onboarding.
- Custom integration work.
- Configuration support.
- Testing and certification support.
- Additional professional services.

Separate standard onboarding from custom development.

### D. Minimum commitment and volume pricing

Where permitted by commercial policy, volume commitments or tiered pricing may be evaluated.

Consider:
- Monthly transaction commitment.
- Monthly processed value.
- Eligible payment-method mix.
- Contract duration.
- Forecast reliability.
- Expected operational support requirements.

Do not promise volume discounts before approval.

## 6. Explain value before discussing fees

Use a value-based conversation.

### Value proposition structure

**1. Operational efficiency**

Understand the current cost of manual billing, payment follow-up, and reconciliation.

**2. Collection visibility**

Explain how structured billing and payment monitoring can help merchants identify successful, unsuccessful, and unresolved billing obligations.

**3. Payment automation**

Explain the supported billing and payment workflow, including applicable recurring authorization and payment method requirements.

**4. Recovery opportunity**

Quantify the value of initially unsuccessful billing that may be recoverable, using actual merchant data and clearly defined observation periods.

**5. Scalability**

Explain how the selected solution pattern may support growth without requiring the merchant to expand manual operations at the same rate.

Avoid claiming a guaranteed increase in collection success or guaranteed operational savings.

## 7. Build a commercial value calculator

Use consistent definitions.

### A. Monthly billing value

\[
\text{Monthly Billing Value}
=
\text{Unique Billing Count}
\times
\text{Average Billing Amount}
\]

### B. Successful billing value

\[
\text{Successful Billing Value}
=
\text{Monthly Billing Value}
\times
\text{Billing-Level Collection Success Rate}
\]

### C. Initially unsuccessful billing value

\[
\text{Initially Unsuccessful Value}
=
\text{Monthly Billing Value}
-
\text{Initially Successful Value}
\]

### D. Recovered value

\[
\text{Recovered Value}
=
\text{Eligible Initially Unsuccessful Value}
\times
\text{Recovery Rate}
\]

### E. Potential processing revenue

For an approved percentage-based fee:

\[
\text{Processing Revenue}
=
\text{Eligible Successful Payment Value}
\times
\text{Approved Fee Rate}
\]

If a fixed fee also applies:

\[
\text{Total Processing Revenue}
=
(\text{Eligible Successful Value}\times\text{Rate})
+
(\text{Eligible Successful Transactions}\times\text{Fixed Fee})
\]

Use the actual contractual fee basis. Some tariffs may have minimums, caps, exclusions, or payment-method-specific rules.

### Important metric distinction

Do not confuse:

- Payment attempt success rate.
- Unique billing-level collection success rate.
- Initial collection success.
- Final collection success after recovery.

For commercial value calculations, use the metric that matches the merchant's actual billing economics.

## 8. Illustrative commercial calculation

Assume the following hypothetical merchant profile:

| Input | Illustrative value |
|---|---:|
| Unique billing obligations per month | 10,000 |
| Average billing amount | Rp250,000 |
| Monthly billing value | Rp2.5 billion |
| Initial billing-level collection success | 90% |
| Initially successful billing value | Rp2.25 billion |
| Initially unsuccessful billing value | Rp250 million |
| Assumed recovery rate | 20% |
| Recovered value | Rp50 million |

The merchant has an illustrative Rp50 million of recovered billing value under the stated assumptions.

This is not a guaranteed outcome. The recovery rate must be supported by observed merchant data or explicitly labeled as a scenario assumption.

### Fee illustration

If an approved percentage-based fee were 2.3%, and the eligible successful payment value were Rp2.25 billion:

\[
Rp2.25\text{ billion}\times2.3\%
=
Rp51.75\text{ million}
\]

This is an arithmetic illustration only, not a DOKU quotation or an indication that 2.3% applies to this merchant.

Before presenting any fee calculation externally, validate:
- Approved fee rate.
- Fee basis.
- Eligible payment methods.
- Fixed fee, if any.
- Tax treatment.
- Refund and reversal treatment.
- Settlement deductions.
- Contractual exceptions.

## 9. Pricing discussion by merchant segment

### SME

Commercial conversation:
- Simplicity.
- Transparent fees.
- Ease of onboarding.
- Reduced manual billing.
- Practical customer payment journey.

Avoid unnecessarily complex fee structures.

### Enterprise

Commercial conversation:
- Scale and volume.
- Integration complexity.
- Operational support.
- Reconciliation.
- Governance and reporting.
- Contractual commitments.

Use a documented business case and approved commercial review.

### Insurance

Commercial conversation:
- Premium collection value.
- Recurring collection profile.
- Failed collection and recovery.
- Payment-method mix.
- Persistency and renewal visibility.

Separate payment attempt metrics from policyholder or billing-level outcomes.

### Education

Commercial conversation:
- Monthly or term-based fees.
- Large customer populations.
- Due-date management.
- Bulk billing.
- Payment status and reconciliation.

### Fitness and membership

Commercial conversation:
- Membership billing frequency.
- Recurring authorization.
- Member registration.
- Payment failure handling.
- Retention and renewal.

Do not promise automatic entitlement or access control unless the required integration is validated.

### SaaS and digital services

Commercial conversation:
- Plan and pricing configuration.
- Billing frequency.
- Customer subscription lifecycle.
- API integration.
- Usage-based billing requirements.

Native metering and advanced usage aggregation should not be presented as available without product validation.

## 10. Handle pricing objections

### Objection 1 — "Your fee is higher than our current provider."

Recommended response:

"Let's compare the complete cost and operating model, not only the headline transaction fee. We should include payment methods, billing operations, reconciliation, integration effort, and the value of collection visibility. Then we can determine whether the proposed solution creates sufficient business value."

Follow-up:
- Are the two providers charging on the same fee basis?
- Are fixed fees included?
- Are payment methods comparable?
- Are settlement and refund treatments equivalent?
- Are platform or integration costs included?

### Objection 2 — "Can you reduce the fee?"

Recommended response:

"We can evaluate the commercial structure based on expected volume, payment mix, contract scope, and implementation requirements. Any adjustment would be subject to DOKU's commercial approval."

Do not offer an unauthorized discount.

### Objection 3 — "We only want the lowest transaction fee."

Recommended response:

"Understood. We should first confirm the exact payment scope and fee basis. We can then compare the commercial offer transparently and identify whether the billing capabilities provide additional value beyond payment processing."

### Objection 4 — "Can we pay only when billing succeeds?"

Recommended response:

"We need to review the approved commercial tariff and contractual fee basis for the relevant payment methods. We will confirm which events are chargeable and how reversals, refunds, and unsuccessful attempts are treated."

### Objection 5 — "Can you guarantee better collection success?"

Recommended response:

"We can help structure the billing and payment workflow and improve visibility into collection performance. Actual collection outcomes depend on customer funding, payment authorization, payment method, merchant data quality, and operational processes. We should establish a baseline and measure the result through an agreed pilot."

## 11. Commercial negotiation guardrails

### Never commit without approval

Do not independently promise:
- Special MDR or transaction pricing.
- Waived fees.
- Minimum volume exceptions.
- Settlement exceptions.
- Custom payment-method pricing.
- Free implementation.
- Custom development at no cost.
- Unapproved support SLAs.
- Product roadmap delivery dates.
- Country-specific availability without validation.

### Negotiation preparation

Before a pricing negotiation, document:

1. Merchant strategic value.
2. Expected payment volume.
3. Expected revenue contribution.
4. Payment-method mix.
5. Implementation cost.
6. Operational support requirements.
7. Competitive context.
8. Merchant forecast confidence.
9. Proposed commercial concession.
10. Approval authority.

### Concession discipline

If a concession is requested, consider whether it can be exchanged for a measurable commitment, such as:
- Higher eligible payment volume.
- Longer contract duration.
- Broader payment-method adoption.
- Additional merchant entities.
- Agreed launch timeline.
- Defined expansion milestones.

Any exchange remains subject to approved commercial policy and formal agreement.

## 17. Final principle

A sustainable FlexiBill commercial proposition balances three dimensions:

**Merchant value** — the operational and financial outcomes delivered to the merchant.

**DOKU value** — payment volume, revenue, strategic relevance, and sustainable contribution.

**Delivery feasibility** — product readiness, integration effort, operational cost, and contractual responsibility.

The best commercial agreement is one where the merchant understands the value, DOKU can deliver the promised solution, and the economics remain sustainable for both parties.
