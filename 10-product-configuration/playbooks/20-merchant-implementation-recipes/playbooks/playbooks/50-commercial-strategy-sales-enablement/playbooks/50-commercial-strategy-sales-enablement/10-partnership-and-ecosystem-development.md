# Partnership & Ecosystem Development Playbook

**Library:** DOKU FlexiBill  
**Audience:** Business Development, Product, Partnership, Sales, Solution Engineering, Technology, Operations, Risk, Legal and Compliance  
**Markets:** Indonesia (ID) and Malaysia (MY)  
**Document type:** Internal partnership strategy and execution guide

---

## 1. Purpose

This playbook defines how DOKU identifies, evaluates, structures, launches and grows partnerships that strengthen FlexiBill's merchant value proposition and ecosystem reach.

Partnerships should help FlexiBill achieve measurable outcomes:

- Acquire qualified merchants and distribution channels.
- Reduce merchant onboarding and integration friction.
- Extend billing and payment use cases through complementary capabilities.
- Improve payment collection, renewal and recovery outcomes.
- Reach industry ecosystems that are difficult to access through direct sales alone.
- Develop repeatable solutions across Indonesia and Malaysia.
- Build a credible foundation for future ecosystem and cross-merchant propositions.

A partnership is not successful simply because an agreement is signed. It is successful when it creates active usage, measurable commercial value, sustainable operations and a positive merchant or customer experience.

## 2. Partnership thesis

### 2.1 FlexiBill ecosystem proposition

FlexiBill should be positioned as a payment-connected billing orchestration capability that can participate in a broader merchant ecosystem.

It connects billing requirements with DOKU payment capabilities and merchant operating processes.

The partnership thesis is:

> Combine DOKU's billing and payment capabilities with partners' distribution, industry access, technology, customer relationships or operational capabilities to create better merchant collection experiences.

### 2.2 Partnership value chain

| Ecosystem layer | Potential partner contribution | FlexiBill / DOKU contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Distribution | Merchant access, referrals, channel reach | Billing and payment solution |
| Technology | Software, ERP, CRM, accounting, platform integration | Billing orchestration and payment connectivity |
| Banking and payment | Banking relationships, funding channels, payment access | DOKU payment acceptance and collection capabilities |
| Industry ecosystem | Vertical expertise, associations, communities | Repeatable billing use cases |
| Implementation | Integration, onboarding, deployment | Product configuration and technical enablement |
| Customer engagement | Communication, customer workflow, service | Billing and payment journey components |

The actual contribution depends on the partner agreement and verified technical capabilities.

## 3. Partnership categories

### 3.1 Distribution and referral partners

Examples:
- Consultants and system integrators.
- Industry associations.
- Business communities.
- Software resellers.
- Merchant service providers.

Potential role:
- Introduce qualified merchants.
- Support initial discovery.
- Help coordinate onboarding.
- Provide market access.

DOKU must define lead ownership, referral eligibility, attribution, commercial treatment and ongoing account ownership.

### 3.2 Technology and integration partners

Examples:
- ERP providers.
- Accounting software providers.
- CRM and customer management platforms.
- Property, education, insurance or membership management systems.
- E-commerce and vertical SaaS platforms.

Potential role:
- Connect merchant systems to FlexiBill.
- Synchronize customer, subscription, invoice or billing data where supported.
- Reduce manual data handling.
- Create repeatable integration packages.

Do not claim a ready-made integration exists until Product and Technology validate it.

### 3.3 Banking and payment ecosystem partners

Potential partners:
- Banks.
- Financial institutions.
- Payment channel providers.
- Relevant payment ecosystem participants.

Potential role:
- Support payment access and collection methods.
- Explore merchant distribution opportunities.
- Support payment-related operating arrangements.

All proposed payment flows must pass payment, risk, legal, compliance and operational review.

### 3.4 Vertical ecosystem partners

Examples:
- Insurance administrators and distribution ecosystems.
- Education management platforms.
- Fitness and membership platforms.
- Travel and hospitality technology providers.
- SME software communities.
- Property and facility management platforms.

Potential role:
- Bring industry-specific workflows.
- Provide access to merchant clusters.
- Help define repeatable billing propositions.
- Support industry adoption and education.

### 3.5 Strategic ecosystem partners

These partners may contribute across multiple layers, such as distribution, technology, payments and industry access.

Strategic partnerships require stronger governance because they can influence product direction, commercial exposure, data responsibilities and long-term platform dependencies.

## 4. Partner qualification framework

Do not prioritize partners based only on brand recognition or the number of merchants they claim to serve.

Evaluate the partner against measurable criteria.

| Dimension | Evaluation questions |
|---|---|
| Strategic fit | Does the partner strengthen FlexiBill's target proposition? |
| Merchant access | Can the partner introduce identifiable, relevant merchants? |
| Use-case fit | Are there recurring, scheduled, installment or repeat-billing needs? |
| Revenue potential | Is there a credible transaction and revenue opportunity? |
| Technical fit | Can the partner integrate using supported methods? |
| Operational readiness | Can both parties support onboarding and production operations? |
| Data and security | Are data access, consent, security and responsibilities manageable? |
| Market relevance | Does the partner have a meaningful role in ID, MY or both? |
| Scalability | Can the model be repeated across multiple merchants? |
| Strategic dependency | Does the relationship create unacceptable concentration or lock-in? |

### 4.1 Suggested scoring model

Score each dimension from 1 to 5.

| Score | Interpretation |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Very weak / not demonstrated |
| 2 | Weak / substantial gaps |
| 3 | Acceptable / requires validation |
| 4 | Strong / evidence available |
| 5 | Excellent / demonstrated and repeatable |

Suggested weighting:

| Dimension | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Strategic fit | 15% |
| Merchant access | 15% |
| Use-case fit | 15% |
| Revenue potential | 15% |
| Technical fit | 10% |
| Operational readiness | 10% |
| Data and security readiness | 10% |
| Scalability | 10% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

Weighted score = sum of (dimension score ÷ 5 × dimension weight).

Suggested decision guide:
- 80–100: Prioritize for partnership design.
- 65–79: Progress with a defined validation plan.
- 50–64: Keep as an opportunity; address material gaps.
- Below 50: Do not prioritize without a clear strategic reason.

These thresholds are proposed internal guidelines, not approved corporate policy.

## 5. Partnership value proposition

A partnership must create value for all relevant participants.

### 5.1 Value to DOKU

- Incremental qualified merchant acquisition.
- Higher billing and payment transaction volume.
- More recurring payment use cases.
- Lower acquisition or implementation cost where the model supports it.
- Stronger vertical distribution.
- Better merchant retention and product adoption.
- A repeatable ecosystem advantage.

### 5.2 Value to the partner

Depending on the model:
- A stronger solution for its merchant or customer base.
- Additional product value without building every capability independently.
- Potential commercial revenue under an approved agreement.
- Improved customer workflow.
- Access to DOKU payment and billing capabilities.
- Joint market development and sales enablement.

### 5.3 Value to merchants

- Less manual billing administration.
- More structured collection processes.
- Better payment access through supported channels.
- More consistent customer billing journeys.
- Improved visibility into collection and payment outcomes.
- Potentially faster implementation through an established partner.

### 5.4 Value to end customers

- Clearer billing information.
- Convenient access to payment instructions and supported payment methods.
- More predictable payment journeys.
- Better communication and support routing.
- Improved transparency on payment status.

Do not promise specific improvements in conversion, collection rates or cost until supported by measured evidence.

## 6. Partnership business models

Select the model that matches the partner's contribution and the operational complexity.

| Model | Description | Main consideration |
|---|---|---|
| Referral | Partner introduces a merchant to DOKU | Attribution and referral terms |
| Reseller | Partner sells an approved DOKU proposition | Sales authorization, pricing and support boundaries |
| Co-selling | DOKU and partner jointly pursue merchants | Account ownership and sales coordination |
| Technology integration | Systems connect to support a workflow | API scope, security and maintenance |
| Embedded distribution | FlexiBill-related capabilities are presented through a partner experience | Product ownership, UX, data and support responsibilities |
| Strategic ecosystem | Multi-dimensional commercial and product collaboration | Executive governance and dependency management |

### 6.1 Commercial principles

Potential commercial structures may include:
- Fixed referral fee.
- Revenue share.
- Transaction-based commercial arrangement.
- Implementation or integration fee.
- Platform or service fee.
- Joint marketing investment.

These are possible models, not approved FlexiBill pricing or contractual commitments.

Every commercial proposal must be reviewed by the authorized DOKU stakeholders, including Finance, Legal, Risk and the relevant commercial owner.

Define:
- Eligible revenue and transaction basis.
- Calculation period.
- Taxes and applicable deductions.
- Settlement and payment terms.
- Refund, reversal and chargeback treatment.
- Referral attribution.
- Contract duration and termination.
- Minimum performance expectations, if any.

## 8. Partner development lifecycle

### Stage 1 — Identify

Build a partner longlist from:
- Existing merchant relationships.
- Industry events and associations.
- Technology ecosystems.
- Banking and payment relationships.
- Merchant referrals.
- Strategic market research.

Output: Partner longlist with category and rationale.

### Stage 2 — Qualify

Apply the partner scoring model.

Validate:
- Merchant access.
- Use-case demand.
- Technical readiness.
- Commercial potential.
- Operational capability.
- Risk and compliance considerations.

Output: Qualified partner opportunity.

### Stage 3 — Discover

Run a structured discovery session.

Understand:
- Partner business model.
- Merchant segments.
- Current workflow.
- Pain points.
- Existing technology.
- Expected partnership contribution.
- Target outcomes.

Output: Partner discovery brief.

### Stage 4 — Design

Define the partnership proposition.

Document:
- Target merchant segment.
- Customer and merchant journey.
- Product capabilities required.
- Integration approach.
- Commercial model.
- Operating model.
- Responsibilities.
- Risks and dependencies.

Output: Partnership solution design.

### Stage 5 — Validate

Before commitment:
- Validate product capability.
- Confirm technical feasibility.
- Review data flows.
- Review commercial assumptions.
- Complete risk, legal and compliance assessment.
- Agree success criteria.

Output: Approved pilot or partnership proposal.

### Stage 6 — Contract and prepare

Complete required agreements and operational preparation.

Typical documents may include:
- NDA.
- Memorandum of Understanding, where appropriate.
- Partnership or referral agreement.
- Commercial schedule.
- Data processing or data-sharing terms, where required.
- Technical integration specification.
- Service and escalation arrangements.

Document names and requirements depend on Legal and Compliance review.

### Stage 7 — Pilot and launch

Start with a controlled scope.

Define:
- Pilot merchants.
- Transaction or billing scope.
- Supported payment methods.
- Test scenarios.
- Support contacts.
- Monitoring.
- Rollback or suspension criteria.

Output: Pilot results and launch decision.

### Stage 8 — Scale and optimize

Expand only when the pilot demonstrates:
- Merchant demand.
- Technical stability.
- Acceptable operational performance.
- Commercial viability.
- Positive customer experience.

Output: Scaled partnership plan.

## 9. Integration and product governance

### 9.1 Integration design principles

The integration model must be selected based on the actual partner workflow and supported FlexiBill capabilities.

Potential patterns:
- API-based integration.
- Host-to-host integration.
- Webhook-driven event handling.
- Batch or file-based exchange where supported.
- Merchant-hosted billing orchestration.
- DOKU-hosted billing automation.

Do not assume all patterns are available to every partner or market.

### 9.2 Key integration questions

| Area | Required validation |
|---|---|
| Merchant identity | How is the merchant onboarded and identified? |
| Customer identity | Which party owns and manages customer records? |
| Billing creation | Which system creates the billing instruction? |
| Plan and pricing | Which system owns pricing and plan configuration? |
| Payment initiation | Which party initiates the payment? |
| Payment status | How is the final transaction status retrieved? |
| Events | Which webhook events are available and required? |
| Retry | Which system owns retry logic? |
| Reconciliation | How are billing and payment records matched? |
| Support | Which party handles each issue category? |

### 9.3 Product governance

Partnership requests must not automatically become product commitments.

Classify every requirement as:

- **Available:** Verified existing capability.
- **Conditional:** Available only under defined conditions.
- **Validation required:** Product or Technology confirmation needed.
- **Gap:** Not currently supported.
- **Future / Strategic:** A possible roadmap or concept, not a released capability.

Product must approve roadmap commitments. Partnership teams must not promise delivery dates or features without authorized confirmation.

## 10. Cross-merchant ecosystem and future opportunities

A longer-term opportunity is to connect billing experiences across multiple merchants.

Potential strategic direction:
- A customer-facing hub for multiple billers.
- Consolidated visibility of bills.
- Selection of individual bills for payment.
- Payment of the oldest eligible invoice.
- One or multiple billing payments in a customer journey.
- Auto-debit for selected eligible bills.
- Merchant-specific privileges and campaigns.
- A common customer identity or access experience, subject to approved design.

A proposed Bill Connector or one-VA-to-multiple-billers concept should be treated as **Future / Strategic** unless formally approved and released.

Before development, validate:
- Biller participation and onboarding.
- Customer consent and identity.
- Bill ownership and authorization.
- Payment routing and settlement.
- Refund and dispute responsibilities.
- Mandate and auto-debit permissions.
- Data sharing and privacy.
- Regulatory and scheme requirements.
- Reconciliation across billers.
- Commercial incentives and liability.

Do not describe this concept as an existing production FlexiBill capability.

## 11. Indonesia and Malaysia market considerations

Partnership strategy must be localized.

| Dimension | Indonesia | Malaysia |
|---|---|---|
| Merchant demand | Validate by vertical and merchant segment | Validate independently by vertical and merchant segment |
| Payment methods | Confirm supported DOKU channels and merchant eligibility | Confirm supported local channels and merchant eligibility |
| Banking ecosystem | Validate bank and payment partner arrangements | Validate local bank and payment partner arrangements |
| Commercial terms | Use approved ID commercial policy | Use approved MY commercial policy |
| Legal and compliance | Obtain ID-specific review | Obtain MY-specific review |
| Integration readiness | Confirm ID environment and capability | Confirm MY environment and capability |
| Operations | Confirm local support and escalation | Confirm local support and escalation |

Do not assume feature parity, identical commercial terms, payment availability or operational processes between markets.
