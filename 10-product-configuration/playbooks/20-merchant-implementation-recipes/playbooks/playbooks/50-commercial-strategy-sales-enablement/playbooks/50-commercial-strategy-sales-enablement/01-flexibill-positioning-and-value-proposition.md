## 1. Executive positioning

### What is FlexiBill?

FlexiBill is DOKU's payment-connected billing orchestration solution. It helps merchants structure billing, manage recurring collection processes, connect billing events with payment execution, and monitor collection outcomes.

FlexiBill connects three business activities:

1. **Billing:** Define what customers owe, how much they owe, and when payment is due.
2. **Collection:** Coordinate payment requests, recurring collection, and recovery processes.
3. **Visibility:** Give merchants a structured view of billing and payment outcomes.

The value is not simply automating a payment. It is helping merchants operate a more consistent, measurable, and scalable billing process.

### Recommended positioning statement

> FlexiBill helps businesses simplify billing and automate payment collection by connecting their billing models, customer payment journeys, and collection operations through DOKU's payment infrastructure.

### Short sales version

> Manage billing, collect payments, and improve visibility — through one connected billing and payment solution.

### Positioning principle

Sell the business outcome first. Explain the product capability second. Discuss integration and configuration third.

## 2. The merchant problems FlexiBill addresses

Many businesses have recurring or scheduled payment obligations but manage the process through fragmented tools.

| Merchant problem | Business consequence | FlexiBill value proposition |
|---|---|---|
| Manual invoice preparation | High administrative workload | Structured billing setup and billing automation |
| Manual payment follow-up | Operational inefficiency | More systematic collection workflows |
| Unpredictable collection timing | Cash-flow uncertainty | Scheduled billing and recurring collection patterns |
| Failed payment without structured recovery | Potential revenue leakage | Better visibility and support for recovery processes |
| Fragmented customer records | Difficult subscription administration | Membership and billing-related customer management |
| Limited collection visibility | Slow management decisions | Billing and payment reporting opportunities |
| Scaling customer volume | More manual work and operational complexity | Repeatable billing and collection processes |

The actual outcome depends on the merchant's configuration, payment methods, integration, operational process, and available product capabilities.

## 3. FlexiBill's solution model

FlexiBill can be discussed through three solution patterns. These are commercial solution patterns, not interchangeable product names.

### A. DOKU-hosted Billing Automation

**Concept:** The merchant uses DOKU-supported billing capabilities to structure billing and collection workflows.

Potential fit:
- Merchants that want to reduce manual billing operations.
- Businesses with repeatable billing schedules.
- Merchants that need a structured membership and billing process.
- Businesses that prefer a DOKU-supported operational model.

Commercial message:

> Start with a structured billing process without needing to build the entire billing orchestration layer independently.

Validate the specific configuration, payment methods, customer journey, and operational requirements before proposing.

### B. API / Host-to-Host integration

**Concept:** The merchant connects its own platform or business system with DOKU-supported FlexiBill integration capabilities.

Potential fit:
- SaaS platforms.
- Digital businesses.
- Enterprises with internal customer and billing systems.
- Merchants requiring system-driven billing and collection workflows.

Commercial message:

> Connect your existing business platform to DOKU's billing and payment capabilities.

Validate API coverage, integration prerequisites, event handling, authentication, testing, and supported lifecycle operations.

### C. Hybrid implementation

**Concept:** The merchant combines DOKU-supported billing capabilities with its own systems and operational processes.

Potential fit:
- Merchants that need a faster initial implementation but retain selected internal controls.
- Enterprises with existing customer platforms.
- Businesses that need a gradual transition from manual or legacy billing.

Commercial message:

> Keep the business systems you already depend on while introducing a more connected billing and payment process.

The exact division of responsibility must be documented in the solution design.

### Important distinction: Merchant Hosted Scheduler

Merchant Hosted Scheduler is a specific implementation pattern and must not be assumed to be available in every market. The documented scope includes Indonesian Business Account; Malaysia availability requires validation.

## 5. Core value pillars

### Pillar 1 — Billing structure

Help merchants organize their billing logic.

Potential capability areas:
- Collections and plans.
- Pricing models such as flat fee, per-unit, tiered, volume, and stairstep, subject to configuration support.
- Billing frequency and duration.
- Billing schedules and due dates.
- Pricing Table and customer-facing payment entry points.

### Pillar 2 — Payment-connected collection

Connect billing obligations with payment execution.

Potential capability areas:
- Supported payment methods.
- Recurring payment and auto-debit arrangements.
- Billing payment links or customer payment journeys.
- API-driven payment initiation where supported.
- Payment status visibility.

Do not imply that every payment method supports every recurring or auto-debit flow.

### Pillar 3 — Customer and membership management

Support customer-related billing administration.

Potential capability areas:
- Member Center.
- Customer and membership records.
- Registration and membership-related workflows.
- Billing-related customer communication.

The availability of an API does not automatically establish that a customer-facing registration frontend is ready.

### Pillar 4 — Collection operations

Help merchants understand and manage billing outcomes.

Potential capability areas:
- Billing and payment status monitoring.
- Failed collection analysis.
- Retry and recovery processes where supported.
- Overdue and grace-period management.
- Operational reporting.

### Pillar 5 — Integration flexibility

Align the solution with the merchant's technical maturity.

Potential approaches:
- DOKU-hosted Billing Automation.
- API / Host-to-Host integration.
- Hybrid implementation.
- Batch or file-based workflows where supported.

## 7. Commercial qualification framework

Use the following questions to determine whether FlexiBill is relevant.

### Billing model
- Does the merchant charge customers repeatedly?
- Is the amount fixed, variable, usage-based, or installment-based?
- Is billing scheduled or triggered by an event?
- Does the merchant have a defined subscription or membership lifecycle?

### Collection process
- How do customers pay today?
- Is payment manual, pay-by-link, auto-debit, or a combination?
- How are failed payments handled?
- How many follow-up attempts are made?
- How long does recovery usually take?

### Customer management
- Does the merchant maintain a customer or member database?
- How are new customers registered?
- Can customers update their payment details?
- How are cancellations, pauses, and renewals handled?

### Technology
- Does the merchant use an ERP, CRM, core platform, or custom application?
- Does it require API integration?
- Can it accept a DOKU-hosted customer journey?
- Does it need batch processing or webhook events?

### Commercial potential
- Number of customers or subscribers.
- Monthly billing volume.
- Average billing amount.
- Payment success rate.
- Failed billing value.
- Recovery rate.
- Expected growth.
- Current operational cost.

## 8. Value quantification

The commercial case should quantify the opportunity rather than rely on generic automation claims.

### Baseline inputs

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Active subscribers | Unique customers with an active billing relationship under the agreed definition |
| Monthly billing attempts | Number of billing or payment attempts in the reporting period |
| Monthly billing value | Value of billing obligations raised in the period |
| Collection success rate | Successfully collected eligible billing value or count divided by the defined eligible denominator |
| Failed billing value | Value of eligible billing obligations not successfully collected by the defined cutoff |
| Recovery rate | Recovered failed billing value divided by the eligible initially failed billing value |
| Average ticket size | Billing value divided by the relevant unique billing count |
| Manual handling cost | Estimated operational cost associated with billing and payment follow-up |

### Illustrative example

Assume a merchant has:
- 10,000 monthly billing obligations.
- Average billing amount: Rp500,000.
- Initial collection success rate: 85%.

| Metric | Illustrative result |
|---|---:|
| Monthly billing value | Rp5,000,000,000 |
| Initially successful value | Rp4,250,000,000 |
| Initially unsuccessful value | Rp750,000,000 |

If 30% of the initially unsuccessful value is subsequently recovered:

- Recovered value: Rp225,000,000.
- Remaining unsuccessful value: Rp525,000,000.

This is an illustrative calculation, not a FlexiBill performance guarantee.

### Measurement discipline

- Separate payment attempts from unique billing obligations.
- Do not count a retry as a new customer or new billing obligation.
- Define the recovery observation window.
- Exclude or separately classify pending and unknown outcomes.
- Distinguish gross billing value from net collected value.
- Apply consistent currency and reporting-period rules.
