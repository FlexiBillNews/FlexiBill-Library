# Sales Playbook and Go-to-Market Execution

**Playbook:** Commercial Strategy & Sales Enablement  
**Document:** 07 — Sales Playbook and Go-to-Market Execution  
**Audience:** DOKU Sales, Presales, Account Management, Partnership, Product Marketing, and Management  
**Status:** Internal working playbook — market, product, and commercial validation required

---

## 1. Purpose

This playbook defines how DOKU teams take FlexiBill to market, from identifying merchant opportunities to acquiring, onboarding, activating, and growing merchants.

It establishes a repeatable go-to-market (GTM) process that connects:

1. Market and vertical prioritization.
2. Merchant prospecting.
3. Opportunity qualification.
4. Discovery and use-case assessment.
5. Solution design.
6. Commercial proposal.
7. Contracting and onboarding.
8. Integration and launch.
9. Adoption and expansion.

**Core principle:**

> Go-to-market success is not measured by the number of merchants who sign. It is measured by merchants who successfully launch, process billing, collect payments, and continue growing.

---

## 2. FlexiBill GTM positioning

### Core positioning

FlexiBill is a payment-connected billing orchestration solution that helps merchants structure billing workflows and connect them with supported payment collection capabilities.

It supports different implementation approaches depending on merchant needs, including DOKU-hosted Billing Automation, API / H2H integration, Hybrid workflows, and eligible batch-based processes.

The merchant proposition should be built around four themes:

| Theme | Merchant problem | Value proposition |
|---|---|---|
| Billing automation | Manual billing and recurring processes | Structure and automate supported billing workflows |
| Payment collection | Fragmented payment processes | Connect billing obligations with supported payment methods |
| Operational visibility | Limited billing and payment status visibility | Improve monitoring and operational control |
| Business scalability | Growing billing workload | Establish a repeatable billing and collection operating model |

Do not position FlexiBill as a complete subscription management, accounting, revenue recognition, or universal payment platform unless the specific capability has been validated.

## 3. GTM objectives

Organize GTM objectives into five dimensions.

| Dimension | Objective | Example measurement |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition | Win qualified merchants | Qualified opportunities and signed merchants |
| Activation | Convert signed merchants into live users | First successful billing and payment |
| Adoption | Expand use of supported capabilities | Active billing use cases |
| Performance | Improve merchant billing outcomes | Billing-level collection success |
| Growth | Increase sustainable business contribution | Payment volume and net revenue |

### Separate pipeline from realized performance

A signed merchant is not necessarily an active merchant.

Track the complete conversion:

**Prospect → Qualified → Discovery → Solution → Proposal → Contracted → Onboarding → Integrated → Live → Active → Expanded**

## 4. Market segmentation strategy

Segment merchants by billing complexity, business model, and commercial potential.

| Segment | Typical billing characteristics | GTM opportunity |
|---|---|---|
| SME | Simple recurring or periodic billing | Fast, standardized onboarding |
| Enterprise | Large-scale billing and integration | Complex solution and account-based selling |
| Insurance | Recurring premiums and renewal cycles | Collection visibility and recovery |
| Education | Term-based or periodic tuition | Bulk billing and payment operations |
| Fitness | Membership and recurring fees | Member billing and renewal |
| SaaS | Subscription plans and recurring charges | Plan-based billing and API integration |
| Travel and hospitality | Deposits, installments, scheduled payments | Structured billing and payment workflows |
| Corporate / B2B | Contractual invoices and periodic collections | Batch and integration-led billing |
| Community and membership | Member dues and periodic contributions | Scalable member billing |

Segment prioritization should reflect validated product fit, not only market size.

## 5. GTM motion by merchant segment

### A. SME-led motion

**Approach:** Standardized, low-complexity sales.

Recommended sequence:
1. Identify merchants with recurring billing needs.
2. Confirm billing frequency and payment requirements.
3. Demonstrate a supported billing workflow.
4. Provide a simple commercial proposal.
5. Complete configuration and onboarding.
6. Support the first billing cycle.
7. Review adoption and payment performance.

Focus on simplicity, time to launch, and operational value.

### B. Enterprise-led motion

**Approach:** Consultative, account-based sales.

Recommended sequence:
1. Identify strategic accounts.
2. Map business and technology stakeholders.
3. Conduct structured discovery.
4. Quantify the business opportunity.
5. Develop a validated solution architecture.
6. Complete product, technical, and risk reviews.
7. Prepare a commercial business case.
8. Agree delivery governance.
9. Execute pilot and rollout.
10. Review account growth opportunities.

Focus on integration feasibility, governance, scale, and measurable economics.

### C. Vertical-led motion

**Approach:** Reusable industry proposition.

For each vertical:
- Define common billing patterns.
- Identify recurring merchant pain points.
- Build a reusable solution blueprint.
- Prepare a relevant demonstration.
- Document supported capabilities and limitations.
- Identify reference customers where permitted.
- Establish industry-specific KPIs.

Avoid presenting an industry-specific workflow as universally available without product validation.

## 6. Merchant acquisition funnel

### Funnel stages and exit criteria

| Stage | Definition | Exit criteria |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Target | Merchant identified | Segment and potential fit recorded |
| 2. Contacted | Initial engagement | Relevant stakeholder reached |
| 3. Qualified | Basic fit established | Business need and potential confirmed |
| 4. Discovery | Requirements assessed | Discovery record completed |
| 5. Solution | Solution design prepared | Feasibility reviewed |
| 6. Proposal | Commercial proposal issued | Proposal and assumptions documented |
| 7. Negotiation | Commercial and contractual discussion | Approved terms agreed in principle |
| 8. Contracted | Agreement completed | Contract and required approvals complete |
| 9. Onboarding | Implementation begins | Owners and plan confirmed |
| 10. Integrated | Required configuration and testing completed | Acceptance criteria met |
| 11. Live | Production activation | First successful billing/payment |
| 12. Active | Ongoing business usage | Agreed activity threshold met |
| 13. Expanded | Additional use cases or volume | Incremental usage demonstrated |

Define the CRM stage and required evidence for each step.

## 7. Merchant qualification

Use the qualification dimensions below.

| Dimension | Qualification question |
|---|---|
| Business need | Is there a real billing or collection problem? |
| Billing model | Does the merchant have recurring or structured billing? |
| Volume | Is the opportunity commercially meaningful? |
| Payment fit | Are required payment methods supported? |
| Product fit | Can mandatory requirements be met? |
| Technical fit | Can the merchant integrate through a supported pattern? |
| Decision authority | Is there access to the decision-maker? |
| Timeline | Is the implementation timeline realistic? |
| Commercial fit | Can the opportunity meet approved economics? |
| Strategic value | Does the merchant create relevant market or ecosystem value? |

### Qualification outcome

- **Proceed:** Strong need and validated solution fit.
- **Proceed with conditions:** Potential fit, but dependencies require closure.
- **Nurture:** Relevant opportunity but not ready.
- **Disqualify:** Mandatory requirements cannot be met or commercial feasibility is insufficient.

## 9. Go-to-market channels

### Direct sales

Use for:
- Enterprise merchants.
- Complex integrations.
- Strategic vertical accounts.
- Large recurring billing opportunities.

### Existing merchant cross-sell

Use DOKU's existing merchant relationships to identify:
- Merchants with manual recurring billing.
- Merchants using payment links for repeated collections.
- Merchants with large membership populations.
- Merchants with fragmented billing and reconciliation processes.
- Merchants planning new recurring products.

Validate account ownership and commercial permissions before approaching.

### Partnership-led acquisition

Potential partner categories:
- Banking and financial institutions.
- Software vendors.
- Vertical SaaS providers.
- System integrators.
- Industry associations.
- Digital platforms.
- Distribution and ecosystem partners.

Define referral, integration, support, data, and commercial responsibilities before launch.

### Digital and campaign-led acquisition

Potential channels:
- DOKU website and product pages.
- Webinars.
- Industry events.
- Merchant education content.
- Case studies.
- Partner campaigns.
- Targeted digital campaigns.

Use approved product claims and current capability descriptions in all external materials.

## 11. Demonstration strategy

A demo should be designed around a merchant problem, not a long list of screens.

### Recommended demo sequence

1. Introduce the merchant's billing problem.
2. Show the relevant billing configuration.
3. Explain the customer or member entry point.
4. Demonstrate the billing and payment journey.
5. Show relevant payment status visibility.
6. Explain exception and recovery handling.
7. Show the operational reporting view, where available.
8. Close with the expected business outcome.

### Demo guardrails

- Use a controlled demo environment.
- Use test data rather than real customer information.
- Do not demonstrate unavailable features as if they are live.
- Distinguish configured capabilities from custom development.
- Explain country and payment-method limitations.
- Document any demo gaps and follow-up actions.
