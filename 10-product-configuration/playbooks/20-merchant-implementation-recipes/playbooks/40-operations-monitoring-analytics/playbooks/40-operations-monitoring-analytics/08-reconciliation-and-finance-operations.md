# FlexiBill Reconciliation & Finance Operations

**Document type:** Finance, Settlement & Reconciliation Operations Playbook  
**Audience:** Finance, Payment Operations, Reconciliation, Merchant Success, Product, Engineering  
**Markets:** Indonesia (ID) and Malaysia (MY)  
**Status:** Proposed operating framework — validate actual DOKU settlement products, contracts, accounting policies, and country-specific requirements before adoption

---

## 1. Purpose

This playbook defines how FlexiBill billing and payment activity should be reconciled with financial records.

It establishes a common framework for understanding the relationship between:

1. Billing obligations generated by FlexiBill.
2. Payment attempts initiated by customers or automated collection.
3. Successful payment transactions.
4. Settlement and payout activity.
5. Fees, refunds, reversals, and adjustments.
6. Merchant financial records and accounting entries.

The objective is to ensure that merchants and internal teams can answer:

- How much was billed?
- How much was successfully collected?
- How much is pending or unresolved?
- How much was settled?
- What fees were charged?
- How much should be paid out?
- Which transactions remain unreconciled?
- Where is the financial discrepancy?
- What is the correct action and owner?

**Core principle:** A successful payment transaction is not the same as a completed settlement, and neither is automatically equivalent to recognized revenue.

## 3. Financial record definitions

| Record | Definition | Primary question |
|---|---|---|
| Billing record | Merchant obligation created through FlexiBill | What is owed? |
| Payment attempt | Individual attempt to collect a billing obligation | Was a collection attempt made? |
| Payment transaction | Payment event with a transaction reference and status | Was payment completed? |
| Settlement record | Financial amount processed for settlement | What amount is being settled? |
| Payout record | Amount transferred or made payable to merchant | What did the merchant receive? |
| Fee record | Applicable processing and service charges | What was deducted or charged? |
| Refund / reversal record | Financial adjustment to an original transaction | What amount was returned or reversed? |
| Accounting record | Merchant or DOKU ledger entry | How is the financial event recorded? |

Do not use these records interchangeably.

## 4. Reconciliation dimensions

A complete reconciliation framework should cover five layers.

### 4.1 Billing reconciliation

Compare:
- Billing records generated.
- Billing amount and currency.
- Due date.
- Billing status.
- Cancelled or expired billing.
- Paid billing.
- Outstanding billing.
- Billing-to-transaction linkage.

Questions:
- Was the expected billing generated?
- Was it generated only once?
- Does the billing amount match the merchant's configuration?
- Was the obligation collected or adjusted correctly?

### 4.2 Payment reconciliation

Compare:
- Payment attempts.
- Final transaction status.
- Successful transaction amount.
- Failed and pending transactions.
- Duplicate successful transactions.
- Refunds and reversals.
- Billing-to-transaction mapping.

Questions:
- Which billing obligations were paid?
- Were there multiple successful transactions for one obligation?
- Are there unresolved transaction outcomes?
- Does the merchant's system reflect the actual payment result?

### 4.3 Settlement reconciliation

Compare:
- Successful transactions eligible for settlement.
- Settlement batches.
- Settlement date.
- Gross amount.
- Fees.
- Refunds and adjustments.
- Net settlement amount.

Questions:
- Which successful transactions are included in the settlement?
- Are there transactions awaiting settlement?
- Do settlement deductions match the applicable rules?
- Are settlement exceptions outstanding?

### 4.4 Payout reconciliation

Compare:
- Settlement amount.
- Payout amount.
- Payout reference.
- Destination account.
- Payout date.
- Payout status.
- Relevant deductions or adjustments.

Questions:
- Was the expected payout initiated?
- Was it completed?
- Does the amount match the settlement calculation?
- Is the payout delayed, rejected, or returned?

### 4.5 Accounting reconciliation

Compare the operational records with approved accounting ledgers and financial statements.

Accounting treatment depends on the legal entity, merchant agreement, accounting policy, and applicable regulations.

FlexiBill operational records should support accounting analysis, but should not be treated as a substitute for an approved accounting ledger.

## 5. Core reconciliation formulas

Use consistent currency, transaction scope, and gross/net basis.

### 5.1 Billing value

\[
\text{Total Billing Value} = \sum \text{Eligible Billing Amount}
\]

Define whether cancelled, expired, adjusted, and disputed billings are included.

### 5.2 Successful collection value

\[
\text{Successful Collection Value}
= \sum \text{Unique Eligible Successful Transaction Amount}
\]

Use a clearly defined transaction grain. Do not sum every successful attempt if multiple records refer to the same financial transaction.

### 5.3 Outstanding billing value

\[
\text{Outstanding Billing}
= \text{Valid Billing Value} - \text{Allocated Payment Value}
\]

Adjust for approved credits, cancellations, and other applicable billing adjustments.

### 5.4 Expected net settlement

A simplified model:

\[
\text{Expected Net Settlement}
= \text{Eligible Gross Settlement}
- \text{Fees}
- \text{Refunds}
- \text{Other Applicable Deductions}
\]

The actual calculation must follow the settlement product and merchant contract. Some deductions may be handled separately or in different settlement periods.

### 5.5 Settlement variance

\[
\text{Settlement Variance}
= \text{Expected Net Settlement}
- \text{Actual Net Settlement}
\]

### 5.6 Payout variance

\[
\text{Payout Variance}
= \text{Expected Payout}
- \text{Actual Payout}
\]

A non-zero variance requires investigation, not automatic classification as financial loss.

## 7. Reconciliation matching strategy

Use a controlled matching hierarchy.

### Level 1 — Exact transaction match

Match using a stable transaction reference.

### Level 2 — Billing-to-transaction match

Match the payment transaction to its Billing ID and merchant reference.

### Level 3 — Settlement match

Match the transaction against settlement records and settlement batch identifiers.

### Level 4 — Payout match

Match settlement totals against payout records and destination account references.

### Level 5 — Controlled investigation

If exact references are unavailable, investigate using approved combinations of:
- Merchant.
- Amount.
- Currency.
- Timestamp.
- Payment method.
- Bank or partner reference.

Do not automatically declare a match based only on equal amount and similar timestamps.

## 8. Reconciliation status taxonomy

| Status | Meaning | Required action |
|---|---|---|
| Matched | Expected and actual records align | No further action |
| Unmatched | No corresponding record found | Investigate missing record |
| Partial match | Some amount or records align | Investigate remaining balance |
| Amount mismatch | Amounts differ | Validate fees, adjustments, or data |
| Timing mismatch | Records appear in different processing periods | Check cutoff and settlement timing |
| Duplicate | Duplicate financial or reporting record suspected | Validate transaction identity |
| Pending | Final financial outcome not yet established | Monitor and investigate |
| Reversed / refunded | Financial adjustment identified | Link to original transaction |
| Exception | Requires specialist review | Assign owner and due date |

Every exception should have:
- Exception reference.
- Amount and currency.
- Relevant transaction and billing references.
- Date detected.
- Root-cause category.
- Owner.
- Next action.
- Resolution evidence.
- Closure date.

## 9. Common reconciliation scenarios

### Scenario A — Successful payment, not included in settlement

Potential causes:
- Settlement cutoff.
- Payment-method-specific settlement cycle.
- Transaction not eligible for the expected batch.
- Settlement data delay.
- Incorrect transaction-to-settlement mapping.

Actions:
1. Verify the final payment status.
2. Locate the transaction in settlement reporting.
3. Confirm the applicable cutoff and settlement rules.
4. Check subsequent settlement periods.
5. Escalate if the transaction remains missing beyond the applicable operational window.

Do not classify it as a loss until the expected settlement process has been investigated.

### Scenario B — Settlement amount differs from gross payment value

Potential causes:
- MDR or processing fees.
- Fixed transaction fees.
- Refunds or reversals.
- Other contractual deductions.
- Currency or rounding differences.

Actions:
1. Recalculate expected settlement using the applicable contract.
2. Validate the fee basis.
3. Link all deductions to their original transactions where possible.
4. Record any unresolved variance.

### Scenario C — Merchant payout differs from settlement

Potential causes:
- Payout timing.
- Multiple settlement batches.
- Bank or destination-account issue.
- Payout rejection or return.
- Additional adjustments.

Actions:
1. Identify settlement batch and payout references.
2. Compare payout amount and currency.
3. Check payout status.
4. Review subsequent payout records.
5. Escalate unresolved differences to Finance / Payment Operations.

### Scenario D — Billing marked paid, transaction missing

Potential causes:
- Incorrect status update.
- Merchant-side state mismatch.
- Webhook or API processing issue.
- Manual status adjustment.
- Missing transaction linkage.

Actions:
1. Verify the billing record.
2. Search transaction records using available references.
3. Review API and webhook logs.
4. Validate whether any manual adjustment occurred.
5. Correct the state only through an authorized process.

### Scenario E — Transaction successful, billing still overdue

Potential causes:
- Delayed event delivery.
- Incorrect billing-to-transaction mapping.
- Merchant-side update failure.
- Subscription or billing state transition issue.

Actions:
1. Confirm successful transaction.
2. Identify the associated billing obligation.
3. Review event delivery and processing.
4. Validate merchant-side state.
5. Correct the billing status through the approved process.
6. Confirm no additional collection is triggered incorrectly.

### Scenario F — Duplicate successful transactions

Actions:
1. Identify all successful transaction references.
2. Confirm whether they relate to one or multiple billing obligations.
3. Review retry and idempotency behavior.
4. Validate settlement and refund records.
5. Escalate potential duplicate collection.
6. Track the authorized resolution.

## 10. Fees and commercial reconciliation

Maintain a fee reference by:
- Country.
- Merchant agreement.
- Payment method.
- Transaction type.
- Fee model.
- Effective date.
- Tax treatment, where applicable.
- Currency.
- Refund or reversal treatment.

### Illustrative fee calculation

Suppose a merchant has:
- Successful payment value: IDR 100,000,000.
- Illustrative MDR: 2.3%.
- Illustrative fixed fee: IDR 4,500 per eligible successful transaction.
- Number of eligible transactions: 100.

Then:

\[
\text{MDR Fee} = 100{,}000{,}000 \times 2.3\%
= 2{,}300{,}000
\]

\[
\text{Fixed Fee} = 100 \times 4{,}500
= 450{,}000
\]

\[
\text{Illustrative Total Fee} = 2{,}750{,}000
\]

This example is for calculation illustration only. The actual fee basis, tax, fee timing, and settlement treatment must be confirmed against the applicable merchant agreement.

## 11. Indonesia and Malaysia considerations

ID and MY must be reconciled independently.

Validate separately:
- Supported payment methods.
- Settlement products and schedules.
- Merchant agreement and fee model.
- Bank and payout arrangements.
- Currency.
- Refund and reversal rules.
- Tax and accounting treatment.
- Payment partner reporting.
- Local operational and regulatory requirements.

Never aggregate IDR and MYR as though they were one currency.

For consolidated management reporting, preserve the original currency and apply an approved FX source, rate, and conversion date.

Merchant Hosted Scheduler is documented for Indonesian Business Account only. Do not assume the same operating or settlement model applies in Malaysia without validation.

## 13. Exception management and aging

Recommended exception aging bands:

| Aging | Classification | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Same day | Newly detected | Assign owner and investigate |
| 1–2 days | Open | Validate source records and partner status |
| 3–5 days | Aging | Escalate to relevant operational owner |
| 6–10 days | High aging | Finance and management review |
| More than 10 days | Critical aging review | Formal resolution plan and accountable owner |

These are proposed internal monitoring bands, not settlement or contractual SLAs.

Segment aging by exception type, market, merchant, payment method, and financial exposure.

## 14. Finance and reconciliation KPIs

| KPI | Definition |
|---|---|
| Billing-to-payment match rate | Eligible billing obligations matched to valid payment records / eligible billing obligations |
| Payment-to-settlement match rate | Eligible successful transactions matched to settlement records / eligible successful transactions |
| Settlement-to-payout match rate | Eligible settlement records matched to payout records / eligible settlement records |
| Unreconciled value | Total value of open financial exceptions |
| Unreconciled transaction count | Number of open transaction exceptions |
| Reconciliation aging | Time from exception detection to resolution |
| Settlement variance rate | Absolute settlement variance / defined expected settlement value |
| Payout variance rate | Absolute payout variance / defined expected payout value |
| Fee variance | Difference between expected and actual applicable fees |
| Refund aging | Time from approved refund initiation to final status |
| Exception resolution rate | Exceptions resolved during period / exceptions eligible for resolution during period |

Always publish the numerator, denominator, reporting period, currency, and matching grain.

A high match rate does not eliminate the need to review high-value exceptions.

## 16. Roles and responsibilities

| Function | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Merchant | Validates billing obligations, commercial terms, and merchant-side records |
| Finance | Owns financial reconciliation and accounting coordination |

Final ownership must be aligned with the approved DOKU operating model and merchant contract.

## 17. Audit trail and control requirements

For each reconciliation cycle, retain the approved evidence needed to reproduce the result:
- Reporting period and timezone.
- Data extraction timestamp.
- Source systems and reports.
- Matching rules and version.
- Currency and FX methodology.
- Gross/net calculation basis.
- Exception list and ownership.
- Adjustment approvals.
- Reconciliation completion status.
- Reviewer and approval evidence, where required.

Apply access controls and data retention policies. Do not circulate unnecessary personal or payment credential data in reconciliation files.

## 19. Final principles

1. Billing, payment, settlement, payout, and accounting are distinct financial stages.
2. Reconcile using stable references wherever possible.
3. Never treat an unresolved payment as a confirmed failure.
4. Do not classify settlement timing differences as losses without investigation.
5. Calculate fees using the actual merchant agreement.
6. Preserve original currency and apply controlled FX methodology.
7. Track financial exceptions with owners and evidence.
8. Keep merchant commercial records and DOKU payment records traceable.
9. Separate ID and MY reconciliation rules.
10. Make reconciliation an ongoing control, not just a month-end activity.

**The objective is a traceable financial chain from every FlexiBill billing obligation to its payment, settlement, payout, and approved accounting treatment.**
